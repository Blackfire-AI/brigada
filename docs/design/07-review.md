# 7. Review policy

After an `implement` job is done and its check passed, the engine creates a review job, placed on an agent of a **different family** than the author's whenever one is available (the family rule below). The reviewer gets the diff (base…branch), the project's reviewer and worker instructions and the task's skills as data (§5.9, §5.10), the task spec and the check result, **never the implementer's summary**, and ends with a verdict block (`approve` | `changes`) plus findings. An approve without substantive findings text counts as `no-verdict` and is retried with another reviewer, so lazy approvals don't inflate the outcome record.

That retry's exclusion of the first reviewer is a preference, not a wall: while at least one other qualifying reviewer is available — ready and not cooling (§8) — the exclusion holds and the retry waits for another reviewer; when none is, the excluded reviewer itself qualifies again, so a review never sits behind another family's reset. The lift reaches the tier choice, the lease's candidates and the cooling hold alike, so the status's held line names the agent the job really waits for, and the `job_agent_picked` event records the lifted exclusion. The lift is spent once: a lifted retry that again ends with no verdict from the same agent parks the task at `needs_foreman`, its reason saying the only available reviewer twice gave no verdict.

**The family rule is `review.family`.** The engine config key beside `review.tier` and `review.effort` (engine config, not `project.yaml`) takes `prefer` (the default) or `require`. Under `require` the rule is never relaxed: an agent of the author's family never qualifies, whatever is available, and a review nobody else can take is unservable with the family-caused reason ending `, review.family=require` (§10).

Under `prefer` the review is placed by three steps, each taken only when the step before it has no available reviewer (ready, not cooling, review class, not capped at 0, §8):

- (a) an agent of another family than the author's,
- (b) another agent of the author's family,
- (c) the author's own agent, in a fresh review session that never sees the author's (below).

The steps outrank the tier (§8): a cross-family reviewer on another tier beats a same-family reviewer on the tier the review asked for. The retry exclusion (above) holds over (b) and (c) as it does over (a). When the author's family is unknown — a standalone branch review with no work job, no trailer and no `default_reviewer` naming it — `prefer` keeps the author's own agent excluded and takes any available review-class agent, as before; step (c) applies only when the author's family is known.

**The placement is labelled.** The lease records the kind on the review job's `job_agent_picked` event beside the pick and tiers: `cross_family`, `same_family`, `same_agent`, or `unknown` (the author's family is not known, never shown as cross-family). `task_reviewing` is written at queue time, before any reviewer is picked, and claims none; only a resumed re-review, whose agent is known then, carries its pick `resumed` and agent.

`brigada history --job N` and the history tool render all four kinds on the pick's line — for example "reviewer agent 3 (same_family: same family as the author, review.family=prefer)" — and `brigada status` names a leased review's placement beside the reviewing task (`review: same_family`, `same_agent` or `unknown`) and under a reviewing plan, leaving `cross_family` unsaid as it leaves engine defaults. A review nobody has leased has no placement: status and notifications give its unservable reason instead, and a verdict that reaches the foreman or the user from a non-cross-family review names the placement once.

**A review's tier and effort are its own.** Reading a diff usually takes less than writing it, so a review job asks for one tier below the task's effective tier by default (heavy → standard, standard → light, light stays light) at medium effort, whatever effort the escalation ladder (§8) has pushed the work to. The config keys `review.tier` (`below`, the default, or `same`) and `review.effort` (`low`, `medium`, `high` or `max`, default `medium`) set that default for the engine; a task's own `review_tier` (`light`, `standard`, `heavy`) and `review_effort`, set in `plan_create`, `plan_amend` (on an added task or in `retier`) and mirrored in the plan file, win over it as given: a task's `review_tier` is the tier asked for, not a step from the task's. Every review job uses them: the first review, a retry after an unusable answer and a resumed re-review alike. Work and fix jobs are unchanged.

The review tier is what §8 treats as the tier asked for: the family rule, the retry exclusion and the cooling fallback apply on top of it, so a review tier with no available other-family reviewer climbs as a work tier does, and the `job_agent_picked` event records the tier asked and the tier served. `brigada status` shows a task's own `review_tier` and `review_effort` beside the task as `review:heavy` and `review@high`; the engine defaults are left unsaid.

`changes` → a fix round (§3 rule) up to `review_rounds` (default 2), then a judgment event.

**A re-review resumes the reviewer's session.** When a review asked for changes and a fix round answered them, the next review is queued in session mode on the agent that wrote that review, resuming that review's session (the session id is on the review job's result). The reviewer's session is not the task key's: the key's stays the author's, so the next fix round still resumes the author's, and the re-review is placed by its agent pin, not by the key's runner affinity.

Its prompt (`internal/prompt`, beside the review template) says this is a re-review:

- the findings are the reviewer's own and are not repeated;
- it carries the fix round's summary (the one place a reviewer sees an author's account, to be checked against the diff, never trusted in place of it) and the check result verbatim;
- the runner writes the fix round's diff alone, the branch's commits since the head the previous review judged, into the diff file (the head is in the author job's result, set by the runner);
- the verdict covers the whole change.

The verdict block is the same contract.

A fresh review on any qualifying reviewer runs instead, with the reason in the `task_reviewing` event, when: the previous reviewer's session id or the judged head is unknown; its agent is gone, broken, cooling (§8), not offering review or failing the family rule against the round's author — under `review.family=require`; under `prefer` the previous reviewer qualifies as the family steps admit it, whatever its family, the author's own agent included; or the task was restarted (a restart, a tier or effort step or an automatic retry starts a new attempt, whose first review is fresh). A re-review whose session the CLI cannot resume (`resume_failed`) is requeued as a fresh review and spends no retry. A review retry after an unusable answer, and a requeue after an unavailable reviewer, are fresh too and carry the full review prompt, not the re-review's.

A resumed review shows in the history: `task_reviewing` carries the pick `resumed` and the agent, and the lease's `job_agent_picked` the same pick with the placement kind, the label of a re-review as of any review.

**A review on the author's own agent never sees the author's session.** A review — first, retry, fresh fallback or re-review — never resumes the task key's session: it runs fresh in a worktree of its own, or resumes only its own earlier review's session, and its session id is never written back to the key, so the next fix round resumes the author's.

**The prompt states the placement.** Review, plan review and re-review prompts are composed at queue time, but the reviewer is picked at lease; the lease rewrites the placement sentence for the kind it records (`internal/prompt` `WithPlacement`): cross-family as composed; same-family — another agent of the author's family — or same-agent — the author's own model in a fresh session with no access to the author's — each asking the reviewer to read as independently as another family would; and, for `unknown`, that the author's family is not known, never that the reviewer is of another family. A re-review says nothing of a cross-family placement and adds the kind's paragraph otherwise.

Family detection: the job record; for `brigada review --branch` without a job, the `Co-Authored-By` trailer. That standalone command creates a one-task plan of class `review`: the runner runs the repo's configured check, and there are no fix rounds unless you ask. Merging is brigada's by rule (§7.1): the land job squashes each approved task onto the target branch and pushes by the push gate, and `brigada land` (§7.1) is the hand-driven form, which stops short of the remote.

## 7.1 Landing approved work: the `land` job, and `brigada land` by hand

**Batch landing, the checked-head suspects, and the land-target sync in this section are design only (issues #134 and #127).** 1.0.0 lands one task per land job — `land_batch_max` (§6.3) is refused as an unknown key until a release implements the batch — a `land_ready` hold carries the command's first output line with no suspect attribution, and no sync job exists: a land target set off the default branch stays current only by the user's own merge. The batch paragraphs below — one job lands a batch, the batch holds, acceptance, running — the checked head and suspects, and the sync paragraphs — keeping the land target current, commits behind — describe the designed behavior, not the shipped one.

### The land job

**Landing is a job.** When a task is `done` and approved and every dependency is landed, the engine's land rule creates a job of kind `land` for the batch of landings that task's landing opens or joins — a batch of one while `land_batch_max` is 1 (below) — leased by a runner like any job (§12.3), with no worker: a runner that advertises git for the project's repo. In order, in a clone of the target branch under the runner's state directory: fetch the target and the task's latest result branch by exact name; squash the task's own diff (three-way, from the diff's base below) as one commit with the message of "The commit message" below; run the project's default check on the result; then, by the push gate (§6.3), push the target, wait for the user, or stop and print the push. It runs where the runner's git credentials are, so the engine still does no git (product §5 rule 1) and a result stays a pushed branch. Landing is **continuous**: per task, not per plan, so a stack lands in dependency order as its tasks finish and the target moves with the plan; the plan's done report names what landed, what waits at a gate, and what the foreman holds. A dependency whose landing still waits at the push gate counts as landed only while the dependent's own landing stacks onto the waiting ref (the `ask` and `never` gates) or once the dependency's push was released or made: under `auto` the dependent waits, for its landing would squash onto the target without the dependency's work. A queued or running land job is live work: the plan closes when the last one has its outcome, so a landing that fails parks its task and keeps the plan open. So is a done task the land rule would land whose landing has not happened and not been declined — no landing record yet, most often because a dependency of its own has not landed, so no land job exists, or the task held at the land gate, its landing waiting on the user's release: the plan stays open until that landing settles — by its job, by the approval that releases the hold, or by its trailer on the branch — and never closes done over a landing that can still fail and park its task under a finished plan. A hold on the push of a landing already recorded is not that hold: the push phase's sits beside a waiting push, and the plan may close over it, the done report naming the task under "waits to push". A hold the user rejected is that decline: the landing will never happen, so it is not live work, and the plan may close, the done report naming the landing declined. A land job that loses its runner too many times fails and parks its task — every member a batch still owns — with a land failure, as one the queue TTL fails does.

### Batch formation

**One job lands a batch.** The land rule stays the one creator of land jobs, and what it creates changes shape: run for a task whose landing is ready, it gathers every landing ready for the same target — across the project's plans, for landing is per task, not per plan — and queues one job of kind `land` for the batch, which runs the opening paragraph's steps once per member, in batch order; how the run handles an outcome partway through, and the batch's one push, is the batch run's own text (below). A landing joins a batch when its task is done, of class `implement`, approved (the paragraph above), every dependency is landed or sits earlier in the same batch, it has no landing record, no land-gate hold holds it and none was rejected, no retry waits on the not-before backoff, its recorded base is not a superseded round (below), and it is not reserved in a live batch. The land-gate rules judge a candidate as they judge a landing today (§6.3): a rule that trips holds the task at the gate instead, and a hold the user releases queues through the same rule, so the released landing joins the next batch like any other.

Members land in dependency order, the order of the stack; tasks with no order between them go by task id, lowest first. The batch takes at most `land_batch_max` members — a project land setting, specified with the land settings (§6.3) — in that order, and the landings past the bound wait for the next batch; a value of 1 is exactly today's rule, one landing per job, the job the rule ran for. The batch job belongs to no one task and to no plan; each member's landing stays its own plan's live work, and a plan closes when its own members' landings have settled, as the opening paragraph provides.

- **One live batch owns every member.** The batch job is keyed `land-batch:<project>` — a project has one land target, so one key covers it — and the one-live-job-per-key rule (§12.1) bounds the formations: two of them racing for the same target cannot both win, for the second finds the key held, queues nothing and leaves its landings for the next batch, so each landing is in at most one live batch.

  Reserving the members is the same transaction as submitting the job: each member task carries the job in `land_batch_job` (§11), a reservation that names a live job or is not ownership, and it holds the member's `task:<id>` key the way a live job holds it — the land rule skips the task, a duplicate creation coalesces (§12.1), `brigada land approve` reports the batch job and queues nothing, as it reports the live job of a held key today (§6.3), the foreman's `land_retry` refuses it as a landing not parked (§12.2), naming the batch job, and `task_restart` is refused as a restart behind a live land job is today, naming the batch job too.

  A landing that turns ready while a batch runs — a dependency landed, a hold released — never joins the running batch and never gets a second land job: the rule finds the target's key held, the task waits for the next batch, and the running batch's outcome re-runs the rule and re-forms it, as a dependency's landing releases dependents today.
- **Each member is released once per batch.** The reservation ends when the landing settles — its record written (by the batch, or by its trailer on the branch, the idempotent read below), a `land_ready` hold taken (below), or a parking refusal or failure — and when the batch job ends without settling it. A job cancelled releases every member still reserved, and the land rule re-forms the next batch; a job failed settles them by its failure's class, and an environment failure — a batch that kept losing its runner — parks every member still reserved under the one cause key, the one wake listing them all (the environment failure bullet below). A lost lease releases nothing: the job keeps its id and its members, the fence turns the dead runner's writes away (§12.3), and the batch runs again under the new lease. Cancelling a member's task while the batch owns it stops the batch job, as cancelling a task stops its live jobs, and the other members land in the next batch; a restart is refused, as above.

  Between the runner's turn on a member and the record of its landing lies an interval no rule assumes closed: ownership is decided in the engine's own transaction, which re-reads the reservation when it writes each member's record — a member the batch no longer owns gets no record from its report, whatever the runner already squashed or checked in the clone, and a record already written is settled, so a cancel or a restart arriving after it meets a landed task, as it does today.
- **`land_ready` gates the batch once, before anything in it lands** (§6.3, below): exit 0 and the batch lands; exit 1 holds — the job completes with the outcome `held` and the command's first output line, nothing squashed, pushed or recorded, and every member without a gate-skip approval is released to a land-gate hold of its own, one needs-you line each carrying the same verbatim first line and suspect line described below, released per member by `brigada land approve`, which re-queues that one landing with the command skipped into the next batch; the engine re-checks every held member on the same 10-minute interval, and a member whose re-check passes is released exactly as an approval releases it — alone, once, into one next batch with its gate skipped, the others waiting for their own re-check; any other exit, a spawn failure or a timeout is not ready — the job completes with that outcome, and every member is released and its landing requeued once on the not-before backoff, no failure and no restart spent, the task staying done and landable. The push phase runs the gate again before its push, for the batch's one push (below); its hold leaves the covered landing records waiting rather than creating new landings.

### Per-task rules and failures

- **The approved head:** the head that lands is the head the approving review judged: the head the author job before that review pushed, as the runner recorded it with the job's result, which the land job's lease carries. A result branch whose fetched head is another — a commit pushed after the review — is refused: nothing is squashed or pushed, and the task parks at `needs_foreman` with a land failure naming both heads, for the foreman to restart it so the new head is checked and reviewed. A result that recorded no head lands its fetched head unchecked.
- **A stale base never lands**: a land job is refused for a task whose recorded base is a superseded round of the dependency it stacks on — the dependency was restarted after the task started and finished again from a new branch (§6.1). Whatever asked for the landing — the rule, or a gate approval's release — nothing is squashed or pushed; and a land job already queued when the dependency is rebuilt — a `land_ready` hold's re-check, a not-ready retry, a landing the rule queued before the rebuild — is checked again when it is leased, cancelled there instead of granted: the task parks at `needs_foreman` with `task_base_stale` naming both refs, the superseded base and the dependency's current branch, and the foreman restarts it so the rebuild starts from the new result. The refusal is transitive in landing order: a chain stacked on a held task waits, and each link is refused in turn until it is rebuilt.
- **An environment failure is one wake per cause**. A landing that fails on the environment — a fetch or clone the network would not carry, credentials the remote refused, a landing setup that would not run, a land job that kept losing its runner — is not about the task's own content: one helper classifies it and derives a cause key, the error class plus the shared remote, and every task the same cause fails parks under that key. The cause's first failure wakes the foreman once, with a `land_env_failed` event whose line and needs-you entry list every task the cause parks; later failures on the active cause attach to that wake — listed in it, never woken separately. The cause clears when a later landing of the project succeeds — the land job's, its push's, or a trailer the runner detected — with a `land_env_recovered` event, or when the tasks it parked leave the queue, restarted, retried with `land_retry` or settled; a failure after that is a recurrence and wakes anew. A failure about the task's own content — a conflict, a failed check, a moved head, a rejected push — parks and wakes per task as always.
- **Conflict:** the job stops, pushes nothing, discards its clone's attempt, and parks the task at `needs_foreman` with the conflicting files. The foreman restarts it with a note (rebase onto the target's new head, resolve), as it restarts a failed task (§5.6); the worker resolves, the check and the review run again, and the task lands on its next approval. A rule never resolves a conflict and never picks a side.
- **The check fails on the landed result:** the same, with the check log as the job's artifact; nothing is pushed. (The case seen in dogfooding: two parallel branches adding the same migration number.) Nothing reruns the check on its own: the foreman reads the log with `check_log` and, when the failure is unrelated to the task's diff, may call `land_retry`, which keeps the approved work and every dependent; a real failure gets the restart a conflict gets. The rerun is the land rule again: no gate passed, no review skipped, no restart spent, the target refetched, the task squashed again, the check rerun on the current target, a head other than the approved one refused, and a push only if the check passes. The check may fail 3 times on one approved head, the original failure and two reruns; `land_retry` then refuses and the task needs `task_restart`. The count is of the task's `task_land_failed` events of a failed check whose job is above its last done work or fix job, so a restart resets it.
- **The record:** the land job's result is the landing record, kept by the engine without a separate post: target, commit, check, pushed or waiting, the exact push command of gate_push never, and an after_land failure. `brigada history` shows it all, and the hand-driven command recognizes a landed task by the commit trailer.
- **Target:** `land_target` (§6.3), the default branch unless set; a staging branch keeps the default branch for the user's own merge. A new task's work starts from the target too (§6.1): its worktree is built on it, and its land job diffs against that base.
- **The diff's base:** the task's recorded base while the branch still sits on it and that base has not landed — a dependent stacked on its dependency's branch is diffed against that branch, so the patch is its own work alone. A base the branch no longer descends from — a dependency that landed as a squash, whose branch never joins the target's history, or a branch rebuilt on the target after a conflict — gives way to the base the squash lands on (the target, or the waiting stack), whose merge-base with the branch is where the task's own work begins; a diff kept on the stale base would carry every change the target made since their merge-base, conflicting in files the task never touched. A base that has landed — recognized by its `Brigada-Task` trailer on the history the squash lands on — goes the same way even when the branch still descends from it, provided the branch's tree holds that history's whole content: the branch may descend from the dependency's old lineage while its tree was rebuilt on it, so the three-dot diff from the lineage starts at the stale merge-base, and the patch is instead the tree diff of the branch against the base the squash lands on, which carries the branch's own change alone. A branch whose tree lacks the base's later changes — another task landed between the dependency's and this one's, and the branch was never rebuilt — keeps the three-dot diff, whose patch holds its own change without reverting them.
- **Retarget off a dead dependency:** the stranded-task retarget (§6.2) is safe only when the done task's own diff is independent of the dead work. Its recorded base must not be the cancelled or failed dependency's result branch (§6.1, `resolveBase`): a task stacked there carries or relies on the cancelled work — the landing diff is taken against the recorded base, above, and would assume it — so the retarget is refused, the refusal saying the task must be restarted on a new base by a follow-up task. Only a task whose base is the land target, or a landed task, may be re-pointed; the freed task then lands exactly its own patch, none of the cancelled work.
- **`after_land`** (§6.3) runs after a push, or after the landing when the push gate is `never`.
- **`land_ready`** (§6.3) runs before anything lands, and the push phase runs it again before it pushes: the gate the landing must pass. Exit 0 lands; exit 1 is the command's own hold — the job completes with the outcome `held` and the command's first output line, nothing squashed, pushed or recorded, and the task holds at the land gate with the needs-you line `held by rule land_ready: <line>`, released by `brigada land approve`; the hold is not final, for the engine re-checks it every 10 minutes with a land job that runs the command again exactly as the landing ran it: the re-check's exit 0 releases the hold itself — the landing proceeds with every other gate applying, and a history event of its own, distinct from the user's release, records the release — its exit 1 keeps the hold with the reason moved to the command's new first line and queues the next re-check, recording nothing when the line is unchanged, and its any other exit keeps the hold and requeues the landing on the not-ready backoff; `brigada land approve` and `reject` cancel a pending re-check, and approve lands exactly once with the gate skipped; exit 2, any other exit, a spawn failure or a timeout is `not ready` — the job completes with that outcome, the command's last exit and a short reason, and the engine requeues the landing on the not-before backoff (retryDelayFor, §3) with the delay grown by the run of consecutive refusals: no failure, no restart spent, the task stays done and landable.

  The next try time and the last exit are kept on the task for the status, which shows the task's landing target state — `ready`, `held by rule land_ready: <line>`, or `waiting (retry at <time>, last exit <n>)`. A grant whose hold an approval released carries the gate as skipped. The hold is not a plan-time rule: it is created from the land job's outcome (§6.3), and `brigada land approve` re-queues the landing with the command skipped.

  The repository ships `scripts/land-ready.sh`, which checks the remote `brigada/landed` head: exit 0 for a missing branch or a successful run for that head; exit 1 for a PR conflict, no run for that head after the grace period, a completed unsuccessful run, or a job that got no runner (a cancelled job with no step run, every other job green) after three attempts, with the reason or run URL on its first line; exit 2 for unknown mergeability, a run still absent within grace or pending, a lookup failure, a job that got no runner while attempts remain (the script reruns it with `gh run rerun --failed`, so `gh` needs write access to Actions) or a rerun gh refuses; the operator enables it with `brigada project set land_ready=scripts/land-ready.sh`.
- **Waiting at the push gate** (`gate_push = ask`): the landing's commit is pushed, fast-forward only, to `refs/brigada/land/<target>`, a ref outside `refs/heads` that holds the newest waiting landing — or waiting land-target sync (below) — with every earlier one beneath it. While that ref is ahead of the target, the next landing is squashed onto it instead of the target, so the waiting landings form one stack in landing order, a dependent on its dependency; a rejected push to the ref is retried from a fresh fetch and squash, as a rejected push to the target is — five attempts in all at the most, a short growing pause between them, the check rerun on every attempt, and a landing that keeps losing the race fails with the rejection and the attempt count, parking the task for the foreman.

  The push phase that `brigada push approve` queues for each fetches the target and the ref, on whichever runner leases it, and pushes the recorded commit: no new squash; the release also re-runs the land rule for each released landing's dependents, as the landing itself did. A commit the target already holds (a later landing of the stack pushed it) counts as pushed; a target that moved outside brigada refuses the fast-forward, and the task parks.

  Under `gate_push = never` the commit waits on the same ref and stacks the same way, so the recorded command — fetch the ref, push it to the target — is exact and runs from the user's own repository; the needs-you queue carries it, and keeps carrying it until the landing's `Brigada-Task` trailer shows on the target, which the engine reads as the push (§7.1, the landing record), or the project is archived (§10).
- **A stale waiting ref** (the landing record below): when a land job drops it, the landings that sat on it — waiting at the push gate, or left to the user under `never` with a command that fetches it — are forgotten and land again, onto a fresh ref, so a recorded command never names a ref that is gone. A landing re-made this way never pushes without the user's yes: when its last landing waited, or was left to the user, and nothing pushed it since, its land job takes the push gate as `ask` while the project's `gate_push` is `auto`, whichever came first, the switch to `auto` or the drop, and it waits for `brigada push approve` again.
- **A restart makes the waiting ref stale** (issue #205): a task restarted while its landing waited at the push gate, or sat local under `never`, keeps no landing — the record would refuse the reworked head a landing of its own, and `brigada push approve` would release the pre-restart commit. The ref still holds the forgotten commit beneath every landing stacked above it, so the target's other waiting and local landings are forgotten with it and land again, and the engine records the target's ref as stale. The next land job of that target carries the record in its grant and deletes the ref, on the remote and in its clone, before it stacks — even when the target could still fast-forward to it — and reports the drop on its completion, which settles it as a positional drop below does; the landing of a job whose grant carried the record clears it too, and a job that fails before it dropped the ref leaves the record for the next one. A landing whose push was already released — a live land job on the task — is left to that job, and a pushed landing is history and stays.
- **Never:** force-push, touch another branch (the waiting ref is brigada's own and no branch), rewrite an existing commit, change the task's state away from `done`, or run with a foreman credential: the job is the engine's, created by the land rule, and the foreman has no tool to create one directly. Its `land_retry` may re-queue, through that rule and every gate, the landing of a task parked by a rejected push, an environment failure or a failed land check under its cap, and refuses a task whose landing is already recorded, pushed or waiting at the push gate: that one wants `task_restart`. So do a conflict, a moved approved head and a stale base. The outcomes are `queued`, `held` at the land gate, `stale_base` and `refused`.

The refusal's conditions are the stale-base rule's above; the base it names for the rebuild follows the rebuild base rule (§6.1): when the dependency's work is on the land target or its chain was abandoned, the `task_base_stale` reason names the land target's tip as the new base instead of the dependency's current branch, and the foreman's restart rebuilds on it.

### Gate evidence and batch holds

**The checked head and suspects.** `land_ready` remains a general command: exit 0 means ready, not necessarily green CI, and exit 1 means hold, not necessarily failed CI. Both call sites record the command identity (path and content digest), remote and checked ref, before/after remote heads, raw exit and first output line.

The runner reads the remote head immediately before and after the command, including an explicit absent value. Different heads, a present/absent transition or a failed lookup make the effective outcome not ready, with no hold, baseline or suspects; the raw exit is still kept. The first landing fetch must agree with the post-command head before the result is used; a mismatch requeues as not ready instead of attributing the command to that newer head. The push phase makes the same comparison before using its result. These observations do not pin an arbitrary command to a ref: if the runner cannot establish which ref the command checks, attribution remains unknown. No script or exit contract changes to provide this evidence.

For attribution the runner recognizes the unmodified shipped `scripts/land-ready.sh` by its known content digest and semantics, not its filename alone. It observes that script's actual remote ref, `brigada/landed`, and attributes it to this landing target only when those refs and remotes agree. With an existing stable head, that script's exit 0 means a successful run for that head and can establish a green baseline; its exit 0 for an absent branch permits landing but establishes no baseline. A modified script or another command still gates by its exit contract but supplies no green baseline or attributed suspects. A ref moved away and back cannot be detected by endpoint comparisons alone; if such movement is known, the evidence is invalidated rather than used for attribution.

The engine stores the last verified baseline for the same remote, target and command identity only when that head is one of its recorded target publications. Each publication records its predecessor head, final head, batch id and ordered members with their individual landing commits; a push of a waiting prefix records the members actually released.

A stable exit 1 names as suspects the members of recorded publications after that baseline through the checked head, in landing order, excluding baseline members and deduplicating tasks. The engine follows its own recorded publication links, never infers membership from git history or blames the batch that is merely waiting to land. A missing baseline, an unrecognized checked head, a changed command identity or a gap in the recorded chain yields `suspects unknown` and no task names. A hold at the baseline itself has an empty suspect list, explicitly shown as no recorded landings since the baseline. These are candidates since the last verified baseline, not a diagnosis. The hold always carries the command's first line verbatim followed by the suspect line, including when the reason is a PR conflict or no run after grace. An unset `land_ready` has no observations, baseline or suspects and changes no landing behavior.

**Deciding a batch hold.** A pre-squash exit 1 gives every member subject to the command its own hold with the same observation and suspect line, and releases those reservations. In a batch without gate-skip approvals this is every member. Approving one member releases only it, exactly once into one next batch; its grant skips `land_ready` as today. The engine's own re-check releases a member the same way: a member whose re-check passes is released alone, once, into one next batch with its gate skipped, while the other holds wait for their own re-check or the user. It can join other eligible members, but its skipped gate does not exempt unapproved members: a mixed batch applies the command's hold only to members without a skip, approved members continue in that same batch if their dependencies are available; an approved dependent of a newly held member is dropped to wait by the batch dependency rule. No approval releases a dependency that remains held.

Rejecting one member declines only its landing and leaves its branch; its dependents keep waiting for their required landing, and the plan closes over a decline only when no other landing remains to settle (§6.3). Other held members stay held. `--and-stop-asking` retains its existing one-setting rule; a batch adds no group-wide setting change. An approval while the member's batch job is live reports that job and queues nothing.

At the push phase, the command runs once for the proposed push, whether it carries a whole batch or an approved prefix. Exit 1 holds that one push and leaves every covered landing recorded as waiting, with the same reason and suspects on its per-member needs-you line; nothing is re-squashed or newly recorded. Approval of one held member releases only its recorded prefix, with the gate skipped for that release, and cannot release later commits. The engine re-checks a held push on the same 10-minute interval, and a re-check that passes pushes the recorded prefix and releases the hold exactly as an approval does, with the same event of its own. Not ready requeues the push with the covered records still waiting. These push holds remain waiting pushes, so they do not reopen a closed plan.

### Running a batch

**Running the batch.** The per-task steps and failures above apply to each member; the batch continues after a member's content failure instead of stopping the whole job.

In its landing clone the runner fetches the target, waiting ref and result branches by exact name, reconciles existing landing trailers, and runs `land_ready` once before the first squash. It uses the target under `auto`, or the waiting stack under `ask` and `never`. For each remaining member in batch order it verifies the approved head and recorded base, squashes that member's own diff as its own commit with its own message and trailers, and runs the project's `default_check` on the entire stack through that commit. Each check artifact names the member and commit it checked. Only a passing member becomes the base for the next member. Local commits alone are not landing records.

After the last passing member the push gate publishes the stack with one fast-forward push; if every member drops out, nothing is pushed and no landing is recorded. The push phase retains its separate `land_ready` run before its push, with the same ready, hold and not-ready outcomes; a refusal leaves the recorded waiting landings waiting and pushes nothing. The command's exit contract is unchanged.

- **A member parks alone.** A squash conflict or a failed local check on the stacked result parks only the member being attempted, at `needs_foreman` with its own land failure and conflicting files or check artifact. The runner discards that member's attempt, restoring the last passing stack. Its direct and transitive dependents in this batch are skipped, left out of the push with nothing recorded for them, and wait as dependents of a held task wait (§6.3); they are not parked. Earlier members and independent later members continue. A moved approved head is likewise that member's own refusal, with both heads named, and its dependents wait by the same rule. Skipped members' reservations end with the batch; when the parked member lands, the land rule releases its dependents into a later batch through the usual eligibility checks. A failed check is attributed to the member just added, whose commit is the only addition since the preceding passing check, even when its change interacts with earlier members.
- **Stale-base refusal stays separate.** A member found to have a superseded recorded base is refused under the stale-base rule above, with `task_base_stale` naming both refs and no commit for it. Its dependents wait, and independent members continue. The batch does not silently retarget or rebuild a stale member; the foreman restarts it as that rule requires. A skipped dependent whose own base becomes stale still has to pass that rule before a later landing.
- **Publishing and recording.** Under `auto` one push publishes all surviving members to the target. Under `ask` and `never` one push publishes them to the waiting ref, in landing order, with the last member at its tip and any earlier waiting stack beneath them. Only after publication is confirmed does the engine write a landing record per member: its target, its own commit and check, pushed or waiting state, exact push command under `never`, and any `after_land` failure. The batch result carries these individual records; a partial report is reconciled idempotently. Push approval is per landing as §6.3 specifies: it pushes the recorded commit without a new squash, carries all earlier waiting commits with it, and leaves later ones waiting. The existing non-fast-forward refusal for an approval still applies; it does not remake recorded waiting commits. `after_land` keeps its existing timing for each landed member.
- **A rejected batch publication retries the remaining batch.** A rejected push to either the target or waiting ref discards the unpublished attempt. From a fresh fetch the runner reconciles any members already published, then re-squashes every remaining member in order and reruns the default check after each, applying the same park-alone and skip-dependent rule. There are at most five push attempts in all, with a growing pause between them. `land_ready` runs once per land job before the first attempt, never again for these retries. After the bound, each surviving member still unpublished parks with its own rejected-push land failure naming the rejection and attempt count; members already landed are reconciled, not parked, and previously skipped dependents remain waiting. A transport or credential failure takes the environment rule instead of being counted as a content rejection.
- **An environment failure partway through stops the batch.** Every still-unlanded member the batch owns, including locally checked members and members not yet attempted, parks under the same cause key, the error class plus shared remote. One `land_env_failed` wake and needs-you entry list them all; an already active cause gains the members without another wake. Members already parked for their own content failure retain that failure; dependents already skipped by that failure keep waiting. A push whose response is lost is reconciled before deciding which members remain unlanded.
- **A lost runner resumes by remote evidence.** The new lease keeps the batch membership and fence described above. Before squashing it fetches the target and waiting ref and matches each member's task and branch-head trailers to its recorded input. A member already on the target is recorded pushed; one on the valid waiting ref is recorded waiting, with its own commit, without another squash. The engine retains per-member parking and skip outcomes as they occur, and the per-member input, check result and publication intent before the push, so this reconciliation preserves earlier failures and can complete records even if the runner dies before reporting them. Unpublished local attempts are discarded and still-owned members without remote evidence are retried in order. Reconciliation precedes both another push and terminal lost-runner failure, so a successful push followed by a lost response cannot park or land its members again. Existing records are idempotent, and every member ends recorded, parked, held, requeued or skipped to wait; none disappears between leases.

### Acceptance outcomes

**Batch acceptance.** These outcomes hold for an ordered batch with independent members as well as dependencies:

- A middle member conflicts → it parks; its dependents in the batch are skipped and wait, not parked; the other members land in one push.
- A check fails immediately after a member's commit is added to the passing stack → that member alone parks with the check artifact naming it; its attempt is discarded, its dependents skip and wait, and independent members continue on the preceding passing stack.
- Under `ask`, all surviving commits wait on the ref in landing order; approving a middle landing pushes its prefix, leaves later landings waiting, and creates no new squash. Under `never`, no automatic push touches the target; the recorded commands and explicit per-landing approvals release the same ordered prefixes. A switch to `auto` alone releases neither stack.
- An environment failure midway through the batch parks all its still-owned unlanded members under one cause key with one wake, including members whose checks already passed locally, while dependents already skipped for a parked content failure stay waiting.
- A runner lost before publication leaves no landing records for local commits and retries every remaining owned member. A runner lost after publication, even before any member record is written, recovers every published member from the remote and records it once. Neither case lands a member twice or loses one.

**Gate and setting acceptance.**

- The remote head changes between the two observations around `land_ready`, or before the first fetch uses its result → effective not ready, no attributed hold and no baseline update, even if the command exits 0 or 1.
- The recognized script exits 0 with the branch absent at both observations → landing proceeds, no green baseline is recorded. An arbitrary command's 0 likewise never becomes evidence of green CI.
- Exit 1 for a PR conflict or no run after grace → each held member shows the first line verbatim and the same suspect line, with no claim that CI failed. With a known baseline and a complete recorded publication chain, only that chain's members after the baseline are named; with no baseline or a head brigada did not publish, suspects are unknown and no tasks are named.
- `land_ready` unset → no suspect tracking or added hold. Approving one member of a batch hold queues only that member once with the gate skipped; rejecting one declines only its landing and leaves the others held. An approval against a live reservation returns its existing batch job. The one-setting behavior of `--and-stop-asking` is unchanged.
- A push-phase hold publishes nothing and keeps the covered members recorded as waiting; releasing a held member publishes only its prefix, leaving later members held.
- `land_batch_max = 1` → one member per job, still keyed `land-batch:<project>`. Omitted → at most five members. Values 0, 21, negative or non-integer → set and import refuse with `land_batch_max` named and preserve the prior setting.
- A batch completion accounts for every granted member exactly once, including skipped dependents as dropped, with no landing record for a drop; an omitted, duplicate or foreign member is refused rather than silently released.

### Land-target sync

**Keeping the land target current.** A land target set off the default branch (§6.3) falls behind it whenever commits reach the default branch outside brigada — the user's own merge of a release, a hotfix pushed by hand — and a task started on the stale base lands work the default branch already holds, conflicting at the PR. The **sync** keeps it current: a land job of its own, no task and no result branch, leased by a runner like any landing, its grant naming the land target and the default branch; it belongs to no plan, so it is never a plan's live work (§7.1) and never holds a plan open.

It runs at the two moments the target is consumed: before a landing's squash, and before any task leases a base from the land target (§6.1; a dependent stacked on a dependency's branch is not held, for its base is not the target). The engine holds those leases until the project's sync is current, one sync per project at a time: a plan's first wave of ready roots (§6.1), concurrent leases from several runners, and the tasks a `plan_amend` adds all queue behind the one running sync and are granted together when its outcome lands.

A landing already in flight when the sync queues is allowed to finish, and the sync is leased only once no landing of the project is in flight, so none pushes the waiting ref under the sync's push; and each of the sync's push attempts re-reads the target and the waiting ref from a fresh fetch before pushing, an attempt that finds the ref ahead of the target completing behind the stack as below instead of merging. The sync merges onto whatever head it finds, retrying a refused push from a fresh fetch up to the same attempt bound a rejected landing push retries.

In its clone the sync fetches both heads and counts the default branch's commits the target does not hold: a target that holds them all is **current** — nothing merges, nothing pushes — and so is a target the remote does not hold yet, for the first landing cuts it from the default branch; otherwise the sync merges the default branch into the target, a true merge commit never a squash or rebase, and brings it to the remote by the push gate (§6.3).

Under `gate_push = auto` the sync pushes the merge to the target itself, a fast-forward like a landing's push; it runs no check (the default branch's commits are reviewed work; the next landing's check runs on the merged tree), no `after_land`, and no `land_ready` gate — it lands no task's work.

Under `ask` the merge waits at the push gate like a landing: pushed, fast-forward only, to the target's waiting ref — alone on it, for no landing stacks under a pending sync — with a needs-you entry naming both branches and the waiting commit, released by `brigada push approve` into the same push phase a landing's approval queues, pushing the recorded merge commit and making no new merge. Under `never` the merge waits on the same ref and the entry carries the exact push command, as a landing's does; it clears when the target holds the merge commit, which the runner's scan reads by the merge's own trailer — `Brigada-Sync: <default>@<head>`, in a message naming both branches — the same read that knows a landing by its `Brigada-Task` (the landing record).

A waiting merge is held by the ask gate as a waiting landing is (§6.3): a switch of `gate_push` to `auto` pushes neither of them, `brigada push approve` releases the merge, and a sync that lands again after its waiting ref went stale waits at `ask` anew rather than pushing under the new `auto`.

A sync whose merge waits at the gate is not finished: it re-queues itself on the not-before backoff, and each attempt fetches both heads and the waiting ref. A target the user moved past the ref — a hand merge of the default branch — makes the attempt drop the ref as the landing record provides and forget the waiting merge, and the sync runs again; a target that already holds the default branch's commits makes it complete current, nothing merged, and the gate opens. A `brigada push approve` whose recorded merge the target can no longer fast-forward to is refused as a landing's non-fast-forward push is; no task parks, for the sync has none — the refusal forgets the waiting merge the same way, and the re-queued sync settles the target.

The sync never merges under a waiting stack: while any landing waits at the push gate — `ask` or `never` — a sync's push would move the target past the waiting ref, every waiting commit would stop being a fast-forward of the target, its recorded push unpushable as recorded, and the landings would have to land again. The sync queues behind the stack instead, and merges once the last waiting landing is pushed or the stale ref is dropped. This is the chosen order because it keeps every recorded commit pushable exactly as recorded — each `brigada push approve` releases the entry it names, a landing's phase pushing that landing's recorded commit with a later one carrying the earlier ones beneath it, and no recorded commit is ever re-made — at the cost of nothing but delay, which the count below makes visible.

A queued sync is still leased while the stack waits: its attempt fetches both heads and the waiting ref, drops the ref when the target has moved past it (the landing record below) — the landings it held are forgotten and land again, and the attempt goes on to merge — and otherwise completes without merging, outcome `behind stack`, re-queuing itself on the not-before backoff and at once when the last waiting landing is pushed or read on the target, so a stack no push releases is never stranded.

Landings and waves that arrive while the stack waits are the one exception to the gate above: the sync does not merge, so they lease on the last sync's standing record — the target has not moved that the engine knows, and a target the user moved by hand is what the queued sync's next attempt reads — and they stack on the waiting ref as this section provides.

The mirror rule holds while a sync is pending — running, conflicting, or waiting at the gate: no landing is leased and no task leases the target as a base; everything queues behind the one sync, for a landing must not squash onto a head the sync is about to move.

A merge conflict, and a held push, before any task runs park nothing per task — there is no landing record to hold. What holds is the gate itself: the sync's outcome is `conflict`, the attempt is discarded in the clone, nothing is pushed, and no task starts from the target and nothing lands until the target is current. The user sees one needs-you line for the project — `land target is <n> commits behind <default> and the merge conflicts in <files>: merge <default> into <target> by hand and push, or clear land_target` — and what releases it is the hand resolution itself: the sync re-queues itself on the not-before backoff (the `land_ready` convention, §6.3), each attempt two fetches and a merge try, so the first attempt after the user's push reads the target as current, opens the gate, grants the queued wave and clears the line.

A held push reads the same before any task runs: the merge waits at the gate with nothing beneath it, the wave holds behind it, and one `brigada push approve` — or, under `never`, the recorded push — releases it.

**Commits behind.** The count a sync keeps is the default branch's commits the remote land target does not hold, read after the sync: 0 when the target is current or the merge was pushed; the earlier count kept when the merge waits at the gate or conflicts, and while the sync queues behind a waiting stack. `brigada status` shows it on the project's line whenever a land target is set and the count is above zero — `land target is <n> commits behind <default>` — and it becomes a needs-you entry (§10), carrying the number, exactly when brigada cannot clear it alone: the merge waiting at the gate, or conflicting. The foreman's opening prompt names both heads — the default branch and the land target, each with the head the last sync recorded, the count too when it is above zero — so a foreman reading the project's history never mistakes the branch the user merges for the branch brigada lands on (§4.2).

### Landing by hand

**By hand.** `brigada land` stays, sharing the squash and message code with the job: for `gate_push = never`, for a branch the user wants on their own machine, and for landing a task by hand. An engineer fetches each approved branch, squashes it onto the default branch, writes a message, reruns the tests and pushes, by hand, per task.

`brigada land <task-id>... [--onto <local-branch>] [--repo URL] [--landed <task-id>]... [--no-check]` does the mechanical part and stops before the push. It refuses a task whose branch head is not the approved head, and warns and goes on for a task whose author's result recorded no head.

One gap remains: the land-note record is not written by the hand-driven command, as the engine has no endpoint for it. The engine adds one read, `GET /v1/tasks/{id}/land` (client and admin tokens only, never the foreman's), which returns the spec, dependencies, approval, the approved head, the author's summary and family; the client runs every git command.

**Who merges.** The hand-driven land is a local convenience the user drives, in the user's own working repository. It writes commits on a local branch and nothing on the remote: the remote stays the user's, and the push is the user's command, which land prints. The foreman never calls it and it is in no MCP set (§12.2): it is a client command, authenticated as the user. Merging is brigada's by default, through the `land` job above, and the person's by rule: the gates and rules of §6.3 are the user's, enforced by the engine, and the foreman can pass none of them.

**What it does.** Run inside the project's repository, on the target branch: the project's default branch, or the local branch named with `--onto`, which must be checked out (land never switches branches; it says `git switch <branch>`). In order:
1. **Preflight, before anything is changed.** Every named task is checked; one failure refuses the whole call and names the task and the reason.
2. **Fetch refs only,** as `brigada diff` does (a fetch from the project's remote of each ref by exact name, no tags, no prune, no checkout): each task's base ref, its latest result branch (`-r<n>` included) and the target's remote counterpart.
3. **Squash in dependency order,** one commit per task, on the target branch (below).
4. **Rerun the project's default check** (§13.1, §6.1) on the result, once, after the last commit, under the environment of any check (§5.8; product §6, "what local gives up").
5. **Stop,** print what it did and the push command (`git push <remote> <branch>`, naming the project's remote), and exit. It never runs it.

**What it refuses.**
- a task that is not `done`, or is not approved: an `implement` task needs an `approve` verdict on its last review (§7); other classes carry no review and no branch worth squashing, so they are not landable;
- a result branch whose fetched head differs from the head the verdict approved (the runner records the pushed head with the result; where no head is recorded, land warns and goes on);
- a dirty working tree (tracked changes, or untracked files that are not ignored), or a merge, rebase, cherry-pick or revert in progress;
- a target branch that is behind or has diverged from its remote counterpart (pull first; being ahead is fine), or that is not the checked-out branch;
- a working directory whose selected remote (`origin`, else the only remote) does not canonicalize (§13.1 item 1) to the task's project, with `--repo` as the same escape `brigada diff` has; with `--repo`, the project's remote is the one whose URL matches it, `origin` preferred, and with no match (a mirror or fork) `origin`, else the only remote;
- a dependency that is neither landed nor in the same call (below);
- no default check, unless `--no-check` is given, the same "never run unchecked" rule as §6.1; `--no-check` is shown in the final report.

**Dependencies and stacks.** Tasks are squashed in dependency order; among tasks with no order between them, in the order given. A task's diff is `git diff --binary <base>...<branch>` for **its own recorded base**, not the default branch, so for a task whose base was its dependency's branch (§6.1, repo and base ref) it is only that task's work. A base is a branch name: the land job fetches `refs/heads/<base>`, so task submit and the plan tools refuse a bare commit SHA base up front, naming it, through one check (`engine.CheckBaseFetchable`) that the land job also runs, so the two cannot diverge.
- *Dependency in the same call:* squashed first, so the target already holds its content and the dependent's patch applies on top.
- *Dependency already landed:* land recognizes it by the `Brigada-Task: <id>` trailer on a commit in the target's history. A dependency landed by hand, or on another clone, carries no trailer, so the user says `--landed <id>`, which skips that task's preflight and is shown in the report.
- *Dependency neither:* refused, naming it, since squashing the dependent alone would apply a patch onto a tree that lacks what it was built on.
- If the dependency's branch moved after the dependent was cut (a later fix round), the dependent's patch may not apply cleanly; that is a conflict like any other.
- A task whose diff is empty makes no commit and is reported.

**Conflicts.** The patch is applied three-way. On a conflict land stops at that task, names the conflicting files, and leaves the tree as it is (conflict markers, index staged) for the user. It never resolves anything, never picks a side, never discards the tree. Commits already made for earlier tasks stay; the report gives the target's starting commit, so the user can return with `git reset --hard <sha>`, printed and never run.

The prepared message for the stopped task is written to a file inside `.git` and its path printed, so the user resolves, runs `git commit -F <file>`, and reruns `brigada land` for the rest, which finds that task landed by its trailer. A git hook that rejects a commit stops the same way (land runs ordinary `git commit`, never `--no-verify`). A failing check undoes nothing: the commits stay, the report names the failure and prints no push command, and the exit code is non-zero.

**The commit message.** Built by the client from data it reads through the API, never from the branch's own commit messages.

The worker may write the message itself: the result block's optional `commit_message` (§5.4), from the task's last author job, carries a subject and a body in the repo's own style, and the land job and `brigada land` use them — the subject its first line, kept whole, the body indented four spaces like the spec and summary parts, so no line of it can read as a trailer or a `#` comment. An absent, empty or subjectless `commit_message` falls back to the built message.

Subject: the task title, one line, never cut or ellipsized (an empty title gives `task <id>`), as the worker's subject never is. A held task that lands, by its trailer or by its land job, leaves the needs-you queue.

Body: a spec summary (the spec's first paragraph, at most 600 bytes) and the worker's summary (§5.4) from the task's last job, at most 2000 bytes. The whole message is at most 4096 bytes. A field over its bound is cut at a character boundary and marked `[truncated]`: unlike §5.9, truncation is right here, since the full text stays in `brigada logs` and the history (§9) and a commit message is a record, not a rule. Trailers:
- `Brigada-Task: <id>` and `Brigada-Branch: <branch>@<sha>`;
- `Co-Authored-By: <model> <address>` for the authoring model family, from the job record (the family detection of §7). The address table carries the five shipped families — anthropic, openai, moonshot, deepseek and zhipu — each with the vendor's conventional noreply address, and the trailer's name is the model id, or the family when the job record names no model; no adapter exposes a display name today, which stays open (§16 item 10). When the family is unknown (no job record, or an agent whose family was never set, such as opencode's default, whose family is empty) there is **no** `Co-Authored-By` trailer and `Brigada-Author: unknown` is added instead; land never invents an identity. A family the table does not list (google, alibaba, meta, mistral, xiaomi, meituan, nvidia) gets the model name alone, without an address, and the name is reported.

**The worker's summary is data.** It is model output: it goes into the message as plain text and is never executed, expanded or interpreted. The message reaches git through a file (`git commit -F`), never a shell string; control characters other than newline and tab are stripped, NUL and CR included; each line of the spec and summary parts is indented four spaces under a heading line, so no line of them can read as a trailer (`Co-Authored-By:`, `Signed-off-by:`, `Brigada-*:`) or as a `#` comment. Only land's own trailers sit in the trailer block. The commit's author and committer are the user's own git identity.

**What it never does.** Never pushes; never force-updates anything; never touches the remote's branches (no delete, no push, no `fetch --prune`); never rewrites commits that existed before the call; never changes the engine's task state, which stays `done`.

### Landing record and trailer scan

**The landing record.** The hand-driven `brigada land` writes no record itself, but the engine sees a landing made outside the land job: wherever the foreman's checkout is freshly fetched — the runner's refresh of the land target (the default branch when none is set or the remote lacks it), on a headless turn or at attach — the runner scans the branch for `Brigada-Task: <id>` trailers and reports each to the engine (`POST /v1/landed`, runner or client token), which records it as the task's landing with the commit that carries the trailer. A task the land job landed is already recorded; either record counts, and the read is idempotent.

This is what lets `brigada status`, the plan listing and the plan's done report tell "already merged" from "not yet landed".

The record also settles a task the land job left unfinished: a task parked at needs_foreman by a land failure (a conflict, a failed check, a rejected push) returns to done and its needs-you lines clear, a landing waiting at the push gate, or left to the user under `gate_push = never`, becomes pushed at the commit that carries the trailer, and the task's dependents are released as a finished task's are. A push rejected as non-fast-forward for a task already recorded landed raises nothing.

A waiting ref `refs/brigada/land/<target>` that the target no longer fast-forwards to — the target moved past it by a hand landing or a cherry-pick — and that no longer holds the target's history is deleted by the next landing job — or by a sync's attempt (below), a queued one's or the waiting sync's own re-queued one, both leased where a landing is not — before it stacks, and the other landings that waited on it are forgotten and land again (a waiting sync's merge is dropped the same way, and its sync runs again); a ref the target can still fast-forward to is untouched by this check, unless a restart made it stale in the engine's record (above), when the next land job deletes it all the same.

## 7.2 Reviewing plans and specs

A plan's tasks are not queued until the plan has been reviewed (§6.2). The review is a `plan_review` job with no task. It is placed by the `review.family` rule (§7) with the foreman as its author: under `prefer` (the default) an agent of a **different family** than the foreman's first, then another agent of the foreman's family, then the foreman's own agent in a fresh session; under `require` only another family. Its lease records the placement kind (`cross_family`, `same_family`, `same_agent`, or `unknown` when the foreman's family is not known) on the `job_agent_picked` event, `brigada status` names a leased plan review's placement under the reviewing plan unless it is `cross_family`, and history renders it.

It runs at the project's `plan_review.tier` (default `standard`) and `plan_review.effort` (default `medium`), so its tier and effort are its own, as a task review's are.

The reviewer runs read-only in a worktree at the head of the land target (§6.3), the default branch when none is set or when the remote does not hold the target yet; its grant carries the land target as its base ref and the default branch beside it, and a note after the plan in its prompt names the ref actually read. A standalone branch review (§7) is unchanged: its diff base is the default branch.

Its input is the plan, every task with its spec verbatim, check, class, tier, effort, skills by name (§5.10) and place in the graph, a summary of the project's other active plans' tasks and branches, and the project's foreman instructions as data, the planning guidance the plan was to follow (§5.9). The contract is the template's; the checks are the project's `plan_review.md` (§5.9), whose default asks eight, each finding citing evidence from the checkout:

- (a) work already on the default branch or in another active plan;
- (b) specs that contradict the code or docs/design.md;
- (c) tasks or plans touching the same files;
- (d) the dependency shape and its width;
- (e) tier and effort that do not fit the task's difficulty;
- (f) a missing or vague check or acceptance criterion;
- (g) a task too big for one job by the task-size guide the project's foreman instructions state (§6.2), to be split into a chain;
- (h) the task's skills (§5.10): open each named `SKILL.md` in the worktree, judge its fit against the spec and cite the description line matched; flag a misfit, and a shape with an obvious skill not picked.

The verdict block (§7) ends the job: `approve`, or `changes` with findings. `approve` runs the approval rules (§6.3) as a no-review plan does. `changes` parks the plan at `needs_foreman` and reaches the foreman as a `plan_reviewed` event with the findings whole under their own 16 KB bound; the foreman's amendment starts the next round, and `plan_accept` accepts the plan as reviewed with a recorded reason. A plan has at most two rounds. The second `changes` proceeds through the same evaluation as `approve`, with its findings recorded and shown.

**Amendments.** An amendment of an active plan that adds tasks or respecs a pending task is reviewed; the round covers only the added and edited tasks. An amendment of a plan awaiting the user's yes is not reviewed: the approval rules judge the whole plan and the user's yes covers it. Cancelling, re-pointing and retiering are never reviewed.

**A reviewer that gives no verdict** (an error, a limit, no block, a malformed block, or an approve without substantive findings) is retried once on another agent, the failed agent excluded, as a task review is (§7). If the retry also gives no verdict, or no other agent can take it, the plan stays `reviewing` with the verdict recorded as `no_verdict` and the reason sent to the foreman (`plan_reviewed`, with a wake). The foreman's amendment answers it: one that adds or respecs tasks starts another round while one is left; one that adds and respecs none (a cancel, a retier) lets the plan proceed through the approve evaluation.

**A reviewer that cannot be had** proceeds through the same evaluation, with the reason recorded: under `prefer` no agent at all offers the review class (the foreman's own agent included, when its family is known), under `require` no agent of another family does, and the reason then ends `, review.family=require` (the plan review skip reason); the job is never leased (the queue TTL), it runs past its hard cap, or it loses its runner `MaxAttempts` times. `brigada recover` requeues the review round of a plan it restores as `reviewing` (§6.4); if no reviewer can take it, the plan proceeds.

**Skipped.** A plan is not reviewed when `plan_review` is off for the project, or when it is exactly one task of the `review` class (the `brigada review --branch` plan).

Events: `plan_review_queued` when a round or its retry is queued, `plan_reviewed` when a round ends or an acceptance lands. Status shows a reviewing plan's round as `reviewing (round N)`, a parked one as `needs_foreman (round N)` in the plans list and under needs you when the foreman does not hold it, and cuts its rendered findings with a pointer to the review job's history.
